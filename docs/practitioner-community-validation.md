# SPONGE practitioner and community validation

Prepared 20 September 2026. This protocol tests whether SPONGE supports an existing planning workflow and communicates its limits. It does **not** test predictive flood accuracy, prove market demand, or request an endorsement.

## Minimum evidence target

- 2 independent stormwater, green-infrastructure, landscape-architecture or resilience practitioners.
- 3 community participants familiar with neighbourhood flooding or local green-infrastructure decisions.
- 1 redacted real workflow example, decision artefact or scenario description that can be used without private data.
- At least 1 product change traceable to observed participant difficulty or a trust concern.

## Consent and safety introduction

> Thank you for reviewing this student hackathon prototype. SPONGE is an exploratory screening tool, not a calibrated flood model or engineering design. This session evaluates the workflow and communication, not you. You may skip any question or stop at any time. Please do not share private addresses, client data or confidential plans. May I take anonymised notes and quote you by role only? I will ask separately before attributing your name or organisation.

Record `yes`, `no` or `notes only` before taking notes. Recording requires separate explicit consent.

## Twenty-minute practitioner session

### 1. Existing workflow — 5 minutes

1. Tell me about the last time you compared stormwater or green-infrastructure options.
2. What decision was being made, and who had to approve or review it?
3. Which maps, models, spreadsheets, site investigations or reports did you use?
4. What was slow, uncertain or repeatedly reworked?

### 2. Unassisted task — 10 minutes

Ask the participant to use the prepared Spring Garden example without a product walkthrough:

1. identify an area of concern;
2. inspect the source-data and model limitations;
3. place or select an intervention;
4. compare baseline and planned flooding;
5. explain the result in their own words;
6. find the evidence or export they would give a reviewer.

Observe task completion, time, requests for help, misinterpretations, trust blockers and missing evidence. Do not teach the interface unless the participant is blocked for more than two minutes.

### 3. Debrief — 5 minutes

1. Which output, if any, could help your current workflow?
2. Which output would you reject or require independent verification for?
3. What could cause a user to become falsely confident?
4. What is the single most important missing input or feature?
5. Who should review a result before it influences a real project?

Do not ask whether the participant “likes” the product or whether it would “save time” until they have described their current process and observed task.

## Fifteen-minute community session

Ask the participant to locate the stated problem, compare a baseline and intervention, and explain:

- what changed;
- whether the change is necessarily accurate for the real neighbourhood;
- which local knowledge or site evidence is missing;
- who should be consulted before an intervention is chosen.

Then ask what community consequence or concern is absent, such as maintenance, construction disruption, accessibility, displacement, safety, standing water or unequal distribution of benefits.

## Predeclared interpretation thresholds

| Measure | Initial threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Independent practitioners describing a recurring comparison workflow | at least 2 |
| Participants completing the comparison without assistance | at least 80% |
| Participants correctly identifying the tool as exploratory | at least 80% |
| Participants treating the result as engineering approval or site eligibility | 0 |
| Practitioner-provided redacted workflow examples | at least 1 |
| Product changes tied to observed evidence | at least 1 |

Missing a threshold is a finding, not a reason to alter the threshold after the sessions.
